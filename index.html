1. Executive Summary

The project is a 10-acre mixed agro-forestry plantation on private agricultural (patta) land in Chittoor or Tirupati districts, Andhra Pradesh, combining high-value white sandalwood, long-rotation teak and an income-generating mango block.

Illustrative allocation for modelling: 4 acres sandalwood, 4 acres teak, 2 acres mango. Exact layout must follow soil tests, water assessment, topography, local climate and written Forest Department guidance.

Mango provides earlier recurring agricultural income; sandalwood and teak are long-term capital assets. Chittoor/Tirupati (Rayalaseema) has suitable semi-arid conditions for these species with irrigation.

2. Project Objectives

  • Establish a legally documented private-land agro-forestry plantation.
  • Create diversified long-term value through sandalwood and teak.
  • Generate earlier cash flow via mango.
  • Maintain complete geo-tagged tree inventory, survey numbers and planting records.
  • Develop compliant felling, possession, transit and sale processes well before the 15–20 year harvest window.
  • If scaled to third-party participants, separate the plantation business from any investment/fund-raising structure and obtain legal opinions before any solicitation.

4. Site Selection and Pre-Plantation Due Diligence

  1. Verify clear title, possession, survey numbers, land classification (must be private/patta agricultural land) and absence of encumbrances.
  2. Obtain current revenue records and maps; retain certified copies.
  3. Conduct soil testing (pH, EC, organic carbon, NPK, micronutrients).
  4. Assess water sources, annual water budget and irrigation infrastructure.
  5. Prepare contour/drainage plan.
  6. Obtain written guidance from the jurisdictional Forest Range Officer / DFO on sandalwood registration, felling, possession and transit requirements.
  7. Use legally traceable planting material; retain nursery invoices and species identification records.
  8. Confirm applicability of Andhra Pradesh Water, Land and Trees Act (WALTA), 2002.

5. Sandalwood: Regulatory Position in Andhra Pradesh

White sandalwood (Santalum album) private cultivation on agricultural land is permitted, but tightly regulated. Key instruments include:

  • Andhra Pradesh Forest Act, 1967
  • Andhra Pradesh Sandal Wood Possession Rules, 1969
  • Andhra Pradesh Sandal Wood and Red Sanders Wood Transit Rules, 1969
  • Andhra Pradesh Forest Produce Transit Rules and related circulars
  • WALTA 2002 tree-felling provisions
Critical points: Do not assume unrestricted future cutting or transport. Felling permission and transit permission are distinct steps. Ownership must be documented from planting through harvest. Approach the local DFO before planting for written clarification.

22. Final Conclusion – Initial Investment & Gross Profit Estimate

Initial investment (excluding cost of land) for a properly established 10-acre project is illustratively ₹40–80 lakh over the full 20-year horizon. The bulk of establishment (Years 0–3) typically falls in the ₹15–35 lakh range depending on site conditions and security standards.

Illustrative gross value/revenue after 20 years (base-case assumptions): approximately ₹6 crore. Conservative case ≈ ₹3.25 crore; upper illustrative case ≈ ₹9.26 crore.

These are gross figures before all costs, taxes, harvesting expenses and risks. Net profit will be materially lower and must be calculated via proper discounted cash-flow analysis.

For Andhra Pradesh (Chittoor/Tirupati), the single most important immediate action is to approach the jurisdictional Divisional Forest Officer before any planting and secure written clarification on the exact sandalwood procedure applicable to the specific survey numbers.
